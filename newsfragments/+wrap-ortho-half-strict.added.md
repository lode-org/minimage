`wrap_ortho_half_strict` in Rust and `minimage::wrap_ortho_half_strict` in C++ wrap one orthorhombic component into the closed interval `[-L/2, L/2]`. The result is the exact nearest image, folded from the exact `fmod` remainder, so values one ulp inside `L/2`, decimal ties such as `0.45` against `0.3`, and large multiples such as `1e308` against `0.5` all stay finite and in range. An exact binary tie keeps the sign of the input. A non-finite component, or a non-finite or non-positive length, returns NaN in both languages.
