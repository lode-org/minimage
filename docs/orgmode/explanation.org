* Conventions and units

minimage is unit-agnostic: coordinates, lattice vectors, origins, and
displacements share one caller-chosen Cartesian length unit, and
squared distances and cutoffs use that unit squared. The single
exception is =from_con_box= (=minimage_cell_from_con_box=), which takes
the three cell angles =alpha=, =beta=, and =gamma= in degrees.
Fractional coordinates from =Cell::fractional= are dimensionless in
=[0, 1)=.

Internally, =Cell= stores the three lattice vectors =a=, =b=, and =c=
as the *columns* of the 3x3 matrix =H=, so Cartesian coordinates are
=r = H s + origin= and fractional coordinates fold via
=s = Hinv (r - origin)=. Constructors that take three separate vectors
(=Cell::from_vectors=, =minimage_cell_from_vectors=) pass columns,
whereas ASE, CON, and vesin constructors (=from_ase=, =from_con=,
=from_vesin=, =PartialCell::from_ase=, and their =minimage_*= C
equivalents) accept row-major 3x3 arrays =[a, b, c]=.

Every displacement function computes the wrapped vector from =p= to
=q=, meaning =wrap(q - p)=. The cell origin cancels in every
displacement and squared distance call.

* Fractional wrap and triclinic cells

A periodic cell is a parallelepiped whose edges are the columns of =H=.
Cartesian coordinates fold to fractional coordinates
=s = Hinv (r - origin)=, wrap each component into the central image,
then map back =r = H s=. Squared engine distance is
=|H wrap(Hinv (q - p))|^2=.

** Orthorhombic cells

Orthorhombic boxes are the diagonal case (Allen and Tildesley; Frenkel
and Smit). Each axis is =d - L floor(d/L + 1/2)= for every image, not
a single subtraction. The half-box tie keeps =-L/2= and maps =+L/2=
onto =-L/2=, matching LAMMPS dump =relDist= in d-SEAMS (Goswami et al.,
*J. Chem. Inf. Model.* 60, 2169, 2020,
[[https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.0c00031][doi:10.1021/acs.jcim.0c00031]]).
=wrap_ortho_half_strict= is the scalar alternative for a closed
interval. It folds the exact remainder =fmod(d, L)= with at most one
addition or subtraction of =L=, which is exact by Sterbenz's lemma, so
it never rounds a quotient: a value one ulp inside =L/2= stays put and
=1e308= against =0.5= gives =0=. An exact binary tie keeps the sign of
=d=, so =+L/2= and =-L/2= are both reachable.
Two positions folded into the box are less than one length apart on
every axis, so the wrap is two selects and the floor runs only past
that. A squared distance needs no sign: for =|d| < L= the wrapped
length is =min(|d|, L - |d|)=, the same square bit for bit with a third
of the selects.

Baseline x86-64 has no rounding instruction, so =f64::round= and
=floor= are libm calls. Rounding half away from zero adds the largest
double below one half, with the sign of the argument, and truncates
toward zero. That is the same integer for every finite double, and the
expansion LLVM itself uses when SSE4.1 is present. The same truncation
runs on every architecture. Truncation of a negative zero is =+0.0=,
so =floor_fast(-0.0)= is =+0.0= and the fractional part of =-0.0= is
=-0.0=. =f64::floor(-0.0)= keeps the minus sign and is not used.
See [[file:simd.org][SIMD paths and dispatch]].

** Restricted and general triclinic cells

A restricted triclinic box (=a= along x, =b= in the xy plane, with
upper-triangular tilts =xy=, =xz=, =yz=) uses the triangular =lamda=
step of LAMMPS =Domain::minimum_image=, HOOMD =BoxDim::minImage=,
OpenMM =applyPeriodic=, and GROMACS =pbc_dx= (Tuckerman, *Statistical
Mechanics: Theory and Molecular Simulation*,
[[https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198825562.001.0001][doi:10.1093/oso/9780198825562.001.0001]]; Wassenaar). It wraps =sz=,
then =sy=, then =sx= using half-away-from-zero rounding (=round_away=),
avoiding a general 3x3 inverse multiply.

A general triclinic orientation (=is_restricted() == false=) computes
two 3x3 products with =Hinv= and =H=. =Cell::reduce_tilts= applies
unimodular GROMACS =correct_box= reduction so that =|xy| <= |lx|/2=,
=|xz| <= |lx|/2=, and =|yz| <= |ly|/2= (tested by =tilts_reduced()=),
preserving the Cartesian lattice. =Cell::to_restricted= rotates a
general parallelepiped into the restricted-triclinic frame (LAMMPS
=define_general_triclinic=).

** Batch fusion

=dist2_many=, =dist2_pairs=, and =wrap_many= are one SIMD pass over
the packed rows for every cell shape: four rows load as three vectors,
subtract while still packed, and transpose once in registers, with no
staging buffer (map fusion in the sense of DaCe and Futhark). An
orthorhombic wrap is per element and does not transpose at all. Each
lane runs the per-pair operations in the per-pair order, so a batch
squared distance equals the per-pair call bit for bit and a wrapped
vector equals it up to the sign of a zero. =dist2_ortho_diffs= keeps
the Highway =BatchPeriodicDistSq= shape: precomputed differences, one
reciprocal per axis, =abs=, then =round=.

Where the processor has AVX-512F and DQ, eight rows go per pass:
two-source permutes transpose them, and the last rows load and store
under a lane mask, so no batch ends in a scalar tail. The intrinsics
are stable from Rust 1.89, which =build.rs= checks; an older compiler
keeps the AVX path. The orthorhombic wrap stays at four rows. It
stores as much as it loads, and a 64-byte store splits a cache line
on all but one alignment in eight.

* Euclidean nearest image and Selling reduction

Smith, *CCP5 Newsletter* 1989: the engine wrap is the Euclidean
nearest image when its length is strictly below half the smallest
face altitude. That is the cutoff regime. Linkcell k-NN has no
cutoff. A hex-prism body diagonal is an engine wrap that is longer
than another image, even when the cell is already tilt-reduced and
Minkowski-reduced.

Past that test, =displacement_euclidean= (=minimage_displacement_euclidean=
in C) finds the closest lattice image from the obtuse superbasis every
three-dimensional lattice has (Selling, *J. Math. Pures Appl.* 1874;
Conway and Sloane, *Proc. R. Soc. Lond. A* 436, 55, 1992,
[[https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1992.0004][doi:10.1098/rspa.1992.0004]]).
A near-parallel basis makes the one-vector Delone step linear in the
reciprocal of the angle, so Lagrange's size reduction (1773; Gauss,
1801) shortens the edges first, keeping the Delone loop within the
1000-step bound used by spglib and Andrews, Bernstein, and Sauter
(*Acta Cryst.* A 75, 115, 2019,
[[https://doi.org/10.1107/s2053273318015413][doi:10.1107/s2053273318015413]]).
The superbasis is cached on the calling thread, keyed by =H=, and is
built on the first query that fails the Smith test. Constructors do
not build it.

The query rounds in three of the four superbasis vectors (Babai,
*Combinatorica* 6, 1, 1986,
[[https://doi.org/10.1007/bf02579403][doi:10.1007/bf02579403]]), then
runs the iterative slicer of Sommer, Feder, and Shalvi (*SIAM J.
Discrete Math.* 23, 715, 2009,
[[https://doi.org/10.1137/060676362][doi:10.1137/060676362]]): while
some relevant vector =r= has =2 |x . r| > |r|^2=, step by =r=. Every
Voronoi-relevant vector of an obtuse superbasis is one of seven
classes =+/- v_S= (Conway and Sloane, 1992), so where the walk stops
=x= is the shortest vector of its coset. A restricted cell with its
tilts inside half an edge starts from the engine wrap instead of
Babai's point. McKilliam, Grant, and Clarkson (*SIAM J. Discrete Math.*
28, 1405, 2014,
[[https://doi.org/10.1137/140952806][doi:10.1137/140952806]]) search
the same subset sums in three rounds of fifteen. A tie keeps the
engine vector.

=dist2_euclidean_many= and =dist2_euclidean_pairs= look the superbasis
up once per call and run the slicer on eight rows per AVX-512 pass: a
row's seven dot products are one lane of eight vectors, the select
tree runs lane by lane, and a lane's Gram row is one =vpermpd= of the
stored rows. The Smith test becomes a lane mask, so the branch that a
single query mispredicts is gone, and a group whose lanes all pass it
skips the slicer. Each lane keeps the scalar order, its step choice,
and its step count, so a batch equals the per-pair call bit for bit.
Without AVX-512 the batch runs the per-pair code with the one lookup.

Across frames a pair seldom leaves its image.
=dist2_euclidean_pairs_warm= keeps each pair's cell-basis shift =n=
from the previous call: subtracting =H n= and testing the seven
relevant vectors replaces the search for a pair still in its Voronoi
cell, and the others take the full search and store their new shift.
Integer shifts stay right when the cell deforms. While the pairs fit
in cache this is 2.2 to 2.8 times faster than the batch; from main
memory, where the shifts are one more stream, 1.15 times. The image is
the one the search finds, and the distance equals it to rounding,
since =H n= is subtracted directly rather than through the engine
wrap.

Nguyen and Stehle (*ACM Trans. Algorithms* 5, 46, 2009,
[[https://doi.org/10.1145/1597036.1597050][doi:10.1145/1597036.1597050]])
Minkowski-reduce a basis (=minkowski_reduce= and =is_minkowski_reduced=,
supporting the 27-image check =displacement_cartesian=), though
Minkowski reduction alone does not eliminate the hex-prism body
diagonal check without searching all 27 combinations. The linked-cell
pair itself is Rapaport's shift, =|q + shift - p|^2=, exposed as
=dist2_shifted_many=. A bin that is an index list rather than a
contiguous slice is =dist2_shifted_indexed=: it gathers a chunk of
those positions into the same kernel. The gather copies every point
first. On a long index list that copy was slower than the inlined
subtract, so the production walk keeps the subtract.

* N-dimensional periodic product metric

=PeriodicSpace= is for feature vectors whose axes are independent:
angles, phases, time-of-day coordinates, or any mixture of circular and
open values. A descriptor contains =Some(period)= for a circular axis
and =None= for an open axis. It is not a replacement for =Cell=: a
triclinic lattice couples Cartesian coordinates through =H=, whereas
this metric is the Cartesian product of circles and real lines.

For a periodic axis, the point representative is in =[0, L)= and a
difference is in =[-L/2, L/2)=. Thus an exact =+L/2= tie maps to
=-L/2=. This is the usual shortest circular separation described by
Mardia and Jupp, /Directional Statistics/
([[https://doi.org/10.1002/9780470316979][doi:10.1002/9780470316979]]). It also matches the toroidal
=boxsize= model in SciPy =cKDTree=, which requires input data wrapped
into =[0, L)=
([[https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.spatial.cKDTree.html][SciPy cKDTree]]).
Varilly's periodic kd-tree uses the same image-search model and avoids
physically duplicating all periodic images
([[https://github.com/patvarilly/periodic_kdtree][periodic_kdtree]]).

The implementation wraps each coordinate independently and sums its
squared separation. This separable form is the best fit for a tree
inner loop: descriptor validation happens once, scalar queries need no
allocation, and batches consume contiguous row-major =f64= slices.

Wrapping one given point or difference uses the same select path as the
orthorhombic engine for nearby values. More distant finite values use
=d - L floor_fast(d/L + 1/2)= while the quotient is representable with
fractional bits, and an exact remainder beyond that range. Corrective
selects enforce =[-L/2, L/2)= after either path. Point wrapping applies
the analogous correction to guarantee =[0, L)= even when division
rounds a tiny negative value onto the upper boundary.

A displacement between two coordinates never subtracts first and wraps
the rounded difference afterwards, because that rounding can exceed the
whole period. With =L = 1e-6=, =0 - (-10)= rounds to =10= and wraps to
zero, but the exact image is about =4.5e-16=. Instead the
displacement is the exact minimum image of =q - p=, rounded once:

- If the raw difference is under =L/2= in magnitude, the exact
  difference is inside the central image and the raw difference is
  already its correctly rounded value.
- If the raw difference is within one period, a two-sum keeps its
  exact rounding error.
- Otherwise each coordinate is reduced with the exact remainder =%= and
  folded into =[-L/2, L/2)=, and a two-sum subtracts the residues.

The remaining fold by one period is exact by Sterbenz's lemma, and the
half-period comparisons are exact on the unevaluated sum. Only the final
addition rounds, so each displacement component is the correctly rounded
exact image. That rounding can produce =+L/2= when the exact image is
less than half an ulp below it. =dist2= sums the squared components.
The =Metric<T = f64>= trait also exposes a zero-sized =Euclidean=
implementation. Only =f64= implementations exist now; the defaulted
scalar parameter leaves room for later =Metric<f32>= implementations
without renaming methods or breaking existing =Metric= bounds. For
an axis-aligned interval, the lower bound is the exact distance to the
nearest translated interval, decided with the same exact pairs and
rounded toward zero before squaring. The squared box bound is the sum of
those one-axis minima, with each square and partial sum rounded down
when more than one axis contributes. It never exceeds the correctly
rounded exact distance, nor =dist2= to any point in the box, so it can
prune a kd-tree or ball-tree node without false negatives. An interval at least one period wide covers its whole
circle. Unlike a generic callable metric in a tree, the explicit box
bound preserves metric-aware pruning; scikit-learn documents that
callable metrics are not supported by =KDTree= because function-call
overhead gives poor performance
([[https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.neighbors.KDTree.html][scikit-learn KDTree]]).

This orthogonal product rule is deliberately simpler than the general
triclinic nearest-image search above. The latter needs a coupled
closest-lattice-point algorithm (Conway and Sloane,
[[https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1992.0004][doi:10.1098/rspa.1992.0004]]); applying independent periods
to a skew cell would be incorrect.

* Partial periodicity

=PartialCell= (=mi_pbc_cell= in C) handles systems periodic along zero,
one, two, or three axes. An open system (zero periodic axes) returns
=q - p=. A wire (one periodic vector =a=) projects onto =a= and wraps
with =s - floor(s + 1/2)=. A slab (two periodic vectors) Lagrange-reduces
its 2D basis and checks the nine neighbouring lattice combinations
around the rounded reduced coordinates, preserving the component normal
to the periodic plane. With all three axes periodic, =PartialCell=
delegates to =Cell= and returns the exact same bits.

* Fixed-point fractions

=Cell::fixed= stores a position folded into the cell as three
fractions on 64 bits, =round(s * 2^52) * 2^12=: the low 52 bits of
=s + 1= are that integer, so a position converts with an add and a
mask, no conversion instruction and no branch, and =fixed_many= does
four or eight at once. The engine wrap of a difference is
then integer arithmetic: =b - a= modulo =2^64=, read as signed, is the
wrapped fraction in =[-1/2, 1/2)=, exactly. The top 52 bits of that
difference convert to a double exactly, and one product with =H= gives
the Cartesian vector, for any cell shape, with no =Hinv= and no
rounding step. That is the exact integer split of the Ozaki scheme
(Ozaki, Ogita, Oishi, and Rump, *Numer. Algorithms* 59, 95, 2012,
[[https://doi.org/10.1007/s11075-011-9478-1][doi:10.1007/s11075-011-9478-1]])
applied to the periodic wrap, and the fixed-point positions of Anton
(Shaw et al., *Commun. ACM* 51, 91, 2008,
[[https://doi.org/10.1145/1364782.1364802][doi:10.1145/1364782.1364802]]).
The wrap is the symmetric residue =a - m floor(a/m + 1/2)= of Ozaki
Scheme II (Ozaki, Uchino, and Imamura,
[[https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08009][arXiv:2504.08009]], 2025) with
the one modulus =m = 2^64=; two's complement computes it, so no Chinese
remainder step is needed. The orthorhombic wrap above is the same
residue with =m = L=. Scheme II splits a product over several small
moduli so that INT8 units compute each part exactly, then rebuilds it
by the Chinese remainder theorem; that pays when many products share
one rebuild, as along the inner dimension of a matrix product. A wrap
has one subtraction per axis and the product with =H= has three terms,
so on a CPU the double-precision units are already the fast path.
Convert positions once per frame; a pair list over them then costs a
subtraction, a conversion, and a matrix product per pair. A fraction
exactly one half apart wraps to =-1/2=, and the distance agrees with
=dist2= to a few units in the last place.

=Cell::fixed32= and the =_fixed32= batches round each fraction to its
top 32 bits: 12 bytes a position instead of 24. Past the second-level
cache a batch moves more bytes than it computes on, about 56 a pair,
so the bytes set the time. On the measured Xeon host the 32-bit batches
are 2.7 to 3.5 times faster than the double batches at 64K pairs, 1.8
to 2.0 times at 16M, and 2 times in cache. The wrap is still exact,
modulo =2^32=, and a displacement is within =2^-32 (|a| + |b| + |c|)=
of the engine wrap, about =7e-9= in a cell 10 long: a tier for
neighbour searches and histograms, beside the double contract.

* Arrays and devices

The Python methods take any host DLPack producer (numpy, PyTorch, JAX,
or CuPy host memory; see also metatensor and metatomic,
[[https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0304911][doi:10.1063/5.0304911]]) and read
contiguous rows where they lie; the result is a numpy array that owns
its buffer through a DLPack 1.0 capsule, so =torch.from_dlpack= shares
it too.

=minimage-burn= puts the wrap on Burn tensors. Burn 0.22 picks the
backend from the device at run time, so one code path serves the CPU,
wgpu, Vulkan, Metal, CUDA, and ROCm, and every step is a matrix
product or an element-wise map that Burn's fusion joins per batch.
Positions folded into =[0, 1)= on the CPU in double precision wrap
exactly on the device in any float width: for =|ds| < 1=, =ds -
round(ds)= subtracts zero or a neighbour of =ds=, which is exact by
Sterbenz's lemma. A single- or half-precision device then rounds only
=ds= and the product with =H=. That is the reduced precision of
kernel_float (Heldens, Netherlands eScience Center), chosen per call,
around an exact wrap. Folding on a single-precision device would lose
the digits of a position far from the origin, so the fold stays on
the CPU.

* Error behaviour

- **Rust**: constructors and slice-validated functions return
  =Result<_, Error>=. =Error::BadBox= covers singular (=|det(H)| < 1e-18=),
  non-finite, or zero-width cells, non-positive lengths in
  =from_con_box= or =dist2_ortho_diffs=, and collinear or non-finite
  periodic basis vectors in =PartialCell::from_ase=, plus non-positive
  or non-finite =PeriodicSpace= periods. =Error::BufferSize=
  covers mismatched slice lengths or out-of-range atom indices in
  =filter_shifted=. =Error::Empty= covers zero dimensions, zero beads,
  empty coordinates, or misaligned bead lengths in =ring_centroid=.
  Once constructed,
  per-pair methods on =Cell= and =PartialCell= are infallible
  (=dist2_shifted_indexed= panics on an out-of-bounds index).
  =wrap_ortho_half_strict= returns NaN, without panicking, for a
  non-finite component or a non-finite or non-positive length.
- **C ABI (=minimage_*=)**: every =int=-returning function returns =0= on
  success and =1= on failure (null pointer, invalid cell, length/index
  error, or integer overflow). On failure, a thread-local NUL-terminated
  UTF-8 error string is stored and returned by =minimage_last_error()=.
  The next successful =minimage_*= call on that thread clears the slot,
  and =minimage_last_error()= then returns =NULL=. =minimage_version()=
  neither reads nor modifies the slot.
- **C++ (=minimage.hpp=)**: wraps C ABI status codes and throws
  =minimage::Error= (carrying =status()= and =what()= from
  =minimage_last_error()=). =Cell::ortho= is =noexcept= and defers
  validation until a query runs; the other constructors validate
  immediately. =minimage::wrap_ortho_half_strict= is =noexcept= and
  returns NaN for the same invalid inputs as the Rust function.
- **Python**: raises =ValueError= on invalid cells, length-3 / 3x3 /
  =(N, 3)= shape errors, or length mismatches; =TypeError= on unsupported
  dtypes, non-CPU DLPack devices, or invalid DLPack objects; and
  =BufferError= on unsupported DLPack capsule versions or re-exported
  buffers.

* Dump bounds are not H

LAMMPS ITEM BOX BOUNDS stores bound spans plus tilt =xy, xz, yz=.
=Cell::from_lammps_bounds= recovers the restricted-triclinic H and
origin:

#+begin_src text
xlo_bound = xlo + min(0, xy, xz, xy+xz)
#+end_src

A CON header may already hold the 3x3 lattice (=readcon-core=,
[[https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22019397][doi:10.5281/zenodo.22019397]]),
or lengths and angles in the crystallographic convention. ASE and vesin
pass rows =(a, b, c)=.

* vesin images

vesin enumerates periodic images. A particle can appear as its own
neighbour through an image, and one neighbour can arrive through
several images. =reduce_pairs= keeps each ordered pair once and drops
the self image.

* Consumers

linkcell re-exports =minimage::Cell= for the fold and the lattice
shift. d-SEAMS ([[https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.0c00031][doi:10.1021/acs.jcim.0c00031]])
calls the C ABI from =lammpsBoxToLcCell=, =periodicDistSq= / =relDist=,
the ortho batch kernel, and the vesin pair collapse.
